## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this Information Security Policy is to establish DTB Nigeria’s formal direction, principles, and mandatory requirements for protecting information assets and managing information security risk in line with ISO/IEC 27001:2022, applicable Nigerian regulatory obligations, and business requirements.

This policy defines management intent and organization-wide commitments for confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information supporting banking operations.

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to all information processing activities within DTB Nigeria ISMS certification scope, including:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan
- All permanent staff, contract staff, third-party users, and service providers with access to DTB Nigeria information assets
- All in-scope technology platforms, including on-premises and cloud services used by DTB Nigeria

Out-of-scope entities (London, Frankfurt, Paris, New York) are excluded from internal application of this policy, except where secure information exchange interfaces with DTB Nigeria are governed by contractual and control requirements.

## 7. Policy Statement and Requirements

### 7.1 Management Commitment

DTB Nigeria is committed to:

1. Establishing, implementing, maintaining, and continually improving an ISMS aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
2. Protecting customer, employee, operational, and regulatory information from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, and loss.
3. Applying risk-based controls proportionate to banking sector threats and service criticality.
4. Meeting applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual information security obligations.
5. Ensuring timely detection, response, and recovery from information security incidents.
6. Providing appropriate resources, competencies, and governance oversight for information security.

### 7.2 Information Security Objectives Alignment

This policy provides the foundation for DTB Nigeria’s measurable information security objectives, including:

- Reduction of high and critical information security risk exposure.
- Improved incident detection and response performance.
- Strengthened resilience of critical banking and digital service platforms.
- Sustained compliance with CBN, NDPA/NDPC, and relevant legal obligations.
- Increased workforce security awareness and policy adherence.
- Improved control effectiveness evidenced through audits, testing, and KPIs.

(Quantified objectives and target metrics are defined in DTB-NG-ISMS-OBJ-009.)

### 7.3 Core Security Principles

DTB Nigeria shall apply the following principles across all in-scope operations:

1. **Need-to-know and least privilege:** Access to information and systems is granted only where required for authorized duties.
2. **Defense-in-depth:** Multiple preventive, detective, and corrective controls are implemented across people, process, and technology layers.
3. **Secure-by-default operations:** Baseline security configurations are mandated for platforms and endpoints.
4. **Risk-based control selection:** Controls are selected and prioritized based on risk assessments and regulatory obligations.
5. **Accountability and traceability:** Security-relevant activities are logged, monitored, and attributable.
6. **Resilience and recoverability:** Critical services shall be protected through backup, continuity, and disaster recovery capabilities.
7. **Continual improvement:** Lessons from audits, incidents, and performance trends shall drive control enhancement.

### 7.4 Mandatory Policy Requirements

#### 7.4.1 Governance and Documentation

1. ISMS documented information shall be created, approved, communicated, protected, and version-controlled.
2. Security roles and responsibilities shall be formally assigned and periodically reviewed.
3. Information security decisions and exceptions shall be documented and approved by authorized roles.

#### 7.4.2 Risk Management

1. Information security risks shall be identified, analyzed, evaluated, treated, and monitored using approved DTB risk methodology.
2. Risk acceptance shall only be performed by authorized risk owners within defined authority limits.
3. Residual high and critical risks shall be escalated through ISMS governance forums.

#### 7.4.3 Asset and Data Protection

1. Information assets shall be inventoried, assigned owners, and classified according to sensitivity and business criticality.
2. Handling requirements shall be defined and enforced per classification.
3. Encryption and key management controls shall be applied based on legal, regulatory, and risk requirements.
4. Data retention and secure disposal shall be performed according to approved schedules and legal obligations.

#### 7.4.4 Identity and Access Control

1. Access rights shall be authorized, provisioned, reviewed, and revoked through controlled processes.
2. Privileged access shall be strictly controlled, monitored, and periodically recertified.
3. Shared or generic accounts are prohibited unless explicitly approved and controlled.
4. Authentication controls for critical systems and digital channels shall be risk-based and robust.

#### 7.4.5 Technology and Operations Security

1. Secure configuration baselines shall be defined and enforced for servers, endpoints, network devices, and security tools.
2. Vulnerability management and patching shall operate to approved severity-based timelines.
3. Logging and monitoring shall cover critical systems and security events, with alert triage and escalation procedures.
4. Malware protection shall be implemented across endpoints and servers using approved enterprise controls.
5. Change management shall include information security impact assessment and approval gates.

#### 7.4.6 Incident Management and Resilience

1. All suspected or actual security incidents shall be reported immediately through defined channels.
2. Incident response shall follow documented procedures including classification, containment, eradication, recovery, and lessons learned.
3. Backup, restoration, business continuity, and disaster recovery controls shall be tested periodically.
4. Significant incidents shall be escalated to executive management and regulatory channels where required.

#### 7.4.7 Supplier and Third-Party Security

1. Supplier security due diligence shall be completed prior to onboarding.
2. Contracts shall define security obligations, confidentiality, incident notification, and right-to-audit or assurance requirements where applicable.
3. Supplier security performance shall be monitored based on risk and service criticality.

#### 7.4.8 People Security and Awareness

1. Staff and contractors shall complete mandatory information security awareness training at onboarding and periodically thereafter.
2. Role-based security training shall be provided for privileged, technical, and high-risk functions.
3. Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, contractual remedies, and/or legal action.

#### 7.4.9 Compliance and Assurance

1. Compliance with this policy shall be monitored via control testing, KPI reporting, internal audits, and management review.
2. Nonconformities shall be recorded, investigated for root cause, and corrected within agreed timelines.
3. Certification readiness and surveillance requirements shall be supported through auditable evidence.

### 7.5 Regulatory and Legal Commitment (Nigeria-Focused)

DTB Nigeria shall maintain and demonstrate conformance with applicable Nigerian legal and regulatory requirements relevant to information security, including:

- CBN cybersecurity and risk management obligations
- NDPA and NDPC requirements for personal data protection
- Cybercrimes Act obligations related to cybersecurity and evidence handling
- Applicable contractual security commitments with counterparties and service providers

Where cross-border exchange with out-of-scope DTB entities occurs, DTB Nigeria shall enforce interface controls and legal/contractual safeguards without extending this policy as an internal governance instrument to those entities.

### 7.6 Policy Exceptions

1. Any exception to this policy requires:
    - documented business justification,
    - risk assessment,
    - compensating controls,
    - defined validity period,
    - formal approval by CISO and relevant risk owner.
2. Exceptions with potential regulatory impact require Compliance/Legal and DPO review.
3. All exceptions shall be recorded and reviewed periodically until closure.

### 7.7 Enforcement

Noncompliance with this policy by employees, contractors, or third parties may result in:

- suspension or removal of access privileges,
- formal disciplinary action under HR policies,
- contractual penalties/remediation requirements for suppliers,
- escalation to legal/regulatory authorities where required.

### 7.8 Communication of Policy

This policy shall be:

1. Communicated to all DTB Nigeria personnel and relevant third parties.
2. Available through controlled internal documentation repositories.
3. Supported by awareness sessions and acknowledgment mechanisms.
4. Referenced by subordinate standards, procedures, and control documents.

### 7.9 Policy Review and Continual Improvement

1. This policy shall be reviewed at least annually and upon significant change.
2. Inputs to review include:
    - audit results,
    - incident trends,
    - KPI performance,
    - regulatory updates,
    - changes in business or technology environment.
3. Policy updates require formal approval and controlled re-issuance.

### 7.10 Assumptions Applied

1. DTB Nigeria has authority to enforce this policy across all in-scope functions and locations.
2. Existing enterprise technologies listed in scope are available to support control implementation and monitoring.
3. Supporting standards and procedures referenced in this policy will be issued and maintained under the ISMS document roadmap.

### 7.5 Measurement and Reporting Method

1. Data sources shall include:
    - SIEM/SOC reports (Microsoft Sentinel, Splunk)
    - EDR and endpoint telemetry (Defender XDR, SentinelOne)
    - Vulnerability and patch management reports
    - IAM and HR lifecycle records
    - Backup/restore and DR test records
    - Compliance obligation trackers
    - Audit and corrective action registers
2. Metrics shall be validated by objective measurement owners before reporting.
3. Monthly objective dashboard shall be presented to ISMS Steering Committee.
4. Quarterly summary shall be escalated to executive management and relevant governance forums.
5. Annual objective performance shall be evaluated during management review.

### 7.6 Performance Thresholds and Escalation

DTB Nigeria uses a RAG performance model:

- **Green:** Objective performance at or above target trajectory.
- **Amber:** Objective variance requiring management attention and corrective planning.
- **Red:** Objective significantly below threshold requiring immediate escalation and corrective action.

Escalation rules:

1. Any objective rated Red for two consecutive months must be escalated by CISO to CEO.
2. Regulatory-impacting objective failures (OBJ-07) require immediate Compliance/Legal escalation.
3. Persistent underperformance must trigger root cause analysis and corrective action records.

### 7.7 Objective Review and Change Control

Objectives shall be reviewed:

- At least quarterly for progress adequacy.
- During management review for strategic suitability.
- Upon significant changes in threats, business priorities, regulations, technology, or major incidents.

Changes to objectives, metrics, or targets require:

1. documented rationale,
2. impact analysis,
3. CISO approval,
4. CEO approval for major strategic/threshold changes,
5. controlled update of this document and related KPI framework.

### 7.8 Linkage to Risk and Continual Improvement

1. Objective performance shall feed risk reassessment and treatment prioritization.
2. Repeated KPI underperformance shall inform:
    - corrective action planning,
    - control redesign,
    - training interventions,
    - resource reallocation.
3. Lessons learned from incidents and audit findings shall be embedded into objective updates for subsequent cycles.

### 7.9 Assumptions Applied

1. Baseline measurements reflect best available internal data at programme initiation as of 01 July 2026.
2. Security telemetry platforms (Sentinel/Splunk/EDR) are operational and capable of producing reliable metrics.
3. Business and technology owners will provide timely, accurate measurement data for objective tracking.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the governance framework for DTB Nigeria’s ISMS, including decision-making bodies, mandates, reporting lines, authority levels, and oversight mechanisms required to ensure effective information security management, regulatory alignment, and ISO/IEC 27001:2022 conformity.

## 3. Scope

This governance structure applies to all ISMS activities within DTB Nigeria certification scope:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

It governs strategic, tactical, and operational information security decisions affecting in-scope business services, technology platforms, and information assets.

Out-of-scope international branches (London, Frankfurt, Paris, New York) are excluded from internal governance forums under this document, except where interface risks require coordination.

## 7. Policy and Procedure (Governance Framework)

### 7.1 Governance Objectives

DTB Nigeria’s ISMS governance structure is established to ensure:

1. Clear decision rights and accountability for information security.
2. Alignment between security strategy, business priorities, and regulatory obligations.
3. Timely escalation and resolution of risks, incidents, and nonconformities.
4. Performance transparency through standardized reporting and KPIs.
5. Effective oversight for continual improvement and certification readiness.

### 7.2 Governance Model Overview

DTB Nigeria applies a **three-tier governance model**:

1. **Strategic Oversight Tier**
    - Board Risk & Compliance Committee (Nigeria Oversight)
    - Focus: risk posture, oversight, executive challenge, strategic assurance
2. **Executive Governance Tier**
    - Managing Director/CEO (Nigeria)
    - Focus: policy approval, resource allocation, high-impact risk decisions
3. **Management and Operational Governance Tier**
    - ISMS Steering Committee
    - Operational Security Working Group
    - Focus: implementation control, remediation, performance monitoring, execution governance

Independent assurance is provided by Internal Audit outside operational governance lines.

### 7.3 Governance Bodies and Mandates

#### 7.3.1 Board Risk & Compliance Committee (Nigeria Oversight)

**Mandate:**

- Provide oversight of DTB Nigeria information security and cyber risk posture.
- Review significant incidents, systemic control weaknesses, and residual high/critical risks.
- Review management’s ISMS performance summaries and readiness posture.
- Challenge adequacy of resourcing and risk treatment progress.

**Minimum Frequency:** Quarterly

**Input Pack:** CISO quarterly report, risk heatmap, major incident summary, audit status summary, regulatory update summary

**Output:** Oversight observations, strategic directives, escalated action requirements

#### 7.3.2 Executive ISMS Accountability (MD/CEO Nigeria)

**Mandate:**

- Approve ISMS policy, objectives, and strategic priorities.
- Ensure adequate financial, technical, and human resources for ISMS operation.
- Approve high-impact risk acceptance decisions outside delegated thresholds.
- Sponsor remediation for material nonconformities and systemic issues.

**Minimum Frequency of Formal Review:** Monthly executive review inputs; quarterly deep-dive

**Output:** Executive decisions, resource approvals, escalation directives

#### 7.3.3 ISMS Steering Committee

**Chair:** CISO (Nigeria)

**Secretariat:** ISMS Programme Manager

**Standing Members:** ERM, COMP, DPO, ITOPS, SOC, DIGI, PROC, HR, BR-OPS representative(s)

**Invitees:** Internal Audit (observer/advisory as needed), legal specialists, system owners (topic-driven)

**Mandate:**

1. Review ISMS implementation and operational performance.
2. Approve tactical remediation and treatment priorities within delegated authority.
3. Review exceptions, overdue actions, and risk acceptance recommendations.
4. Monitor objective/KPI progress and corrective action closure.
5. Oversee certification readiness activities and evidence completeness.

**Minimum Frequency:** Monthly

**Quorum Requirement:** Chair + minimum 60% of standing members, including ITOPS or SOC, and COMP or DPO

**Output:** Approved actions, escalations, exception decisions, timeline commitments

#### 7.3.4 Operational Security Working Group

**Lead:** SOC Head and ISMS Programme Manager (joint facilitation)

**Participants:** Technical and process control owners from IT, security, digital channels, branch operations, and vendor/security assurance functions

**Mandate:**

- Execute and track control implementation tasks.
- Resolve operational blockers for vulnerability, patching, access reviews, monitoring coverage, and evidence quality.
- Prepare escalations for Steering Committee where required.

**Minimum Frequency:** Bi-weekly

**Output:** Updated execution trackers, blocker logs, escalation notes

#### 7.3.5 Management Review Forum (ISO Clause 9.3)

**Chair:** Managing Director/CEO (Nigeria)

**Coordinator:** CISO

**Participants:** Executive and key functional leaders per management review procedure

**Mandate:**

- Formally assess ISMS suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness.
- Evaluate trends, changes, findings, objectives, incidents, and improvement opportunities.
- Issue strategic decisions and actions for continual improvement.

**Minimum Frequency:** At least annually (with interim reviews as required)

### 7.4 Governance Decision Rights and Authority Matrix (Summary)

| **Decision Area** | **Primary Decision Authority** | **Consulted Functions** | **Escalation Path** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ISMS policy approval | CEO | CISO, COMP, DPO | BRCC oversight |
| ISMS objectives and thresholds | CEO | CISO, ERM, ITOPS, SOC, COMP | BRCC oversight for strategic risk implications |
| High-impact risk acceptance | CEO | CISO, ERM, COMP, DPO (if privacy related) | BRCC review where material |
| Operational control prioritization | ISMS Steering Committee (Chair: CISO) | ITOPS, SOC, DIGI, ERM, COMP | CEO if resource or strategic conflict |
| Regulatory response decisions | COMP (legal interpretation) + CEO/CISO (execution approval) | DPO, ERM, ITOPS/SOC as applicable | BRCC where material |
| Major incident crisis governance | CISO and CEO (joint) | SOC, ITOPS, COMP, DPO, ERM | BRCC notification/oversight |
| Supplier security exception decisions | CISO + PROC (joint within delegated limit) | COMP, ERM, ITOPS | CEO for high-risk exceptions |
| Corrective action closure acceptance (major NC) | CISO | Process owner, IA (verification), ERM/COMP as needed | CEO/BRCC for recurring systemic issues |

### 7.5 Governance Reporting Framework

#### 7.5.1 Monthly ISMS Governance Dashboard (Minimum Content)

1. ISMS objective status (RAG)
2. Risk posture summary (top risks and trend movement)
3. Incident metrics (volume, severity, MTTD, MTTR, root cause themes)
4. Vulnerability and patch compliance status
5. IAM recertification and privileged access governance status
6. Supplier security assurance status
7. Audit findings and corrective action closure status
8. Regulatory compliance status and upcoming obligations
9. Certification readiness index and evidence completeness

#### 7.5.2 Quarterly Executive/Board Oversight Pack (Minimum Content)

1. Strategic risk and control maturity trend
2. Material incidents and lessons learned
3. Regulatory posture and material obligations status
4. Internal audit outcomes and major nonconformity trend
5. Resource adequacy and investment needs
6. Management decisions required

### 7.6 Escalation and Issue Management Governance

1. **Immediate escalation triggers (within 24 hours):**
    - Major security incident with customer or critical service impact
    - Regulatory-reportable security event
    - Significant data compromise indicators
    - Material control failure affecting critical systems
2. **Expedited escalation triggers (within 2 business days):**
    - High/critical risk without approved treatment owner
    - Repeated KPI red status for critical objectives
    - Overdue corrective actions with systemic impact
3. **Escalation channels:**
    - Operational to Steering Committee
    - Steering Committee to CEO
    - CEO to BRCC oversight where strategic or systemic

All escalations shall be documented with owner, due date, and status.

### 7.7 Governance Integration with Enterprise Functions

DTB Nigeria integrates ISMS governance with:

- Enterprise Risk governance (risk appetite, risk reporting alignment)
- Compliance governance (legal and regulatory interpretation)
- Technology governance (change, architecture, resilience decisions)
- HR governance (competence and disciplinary controls)
- Procurement governance (third-party risk and contractual controls)
- Internal Audit governance (independent assurance and follow-up)

This integration avoids duplicated controls and ensures unified decision-making across assurance domains.

### 7.8 Governance Records and Evidence Requirements

Each governance forum shall maintain:

1. Approved Terms of Reference (ToR)
2. Membership and role definitions
3. Annual meeting calendar
4. Agenda, minutes, action logs, and decision records
5. Escalation records and closure evidence
6. Performance dashboard archives
7. Attendance logs and quorum evidence

### 7.9 Governance Effectiveness Review

Governance effectiveness shall be evaluated through:

- Internal audit reviews of governance process operation
- Timeliness and closure quality of governance actions
- Consistency of decision traceability
- Improvement in objective/KPI trends
- Management review outcomes and resulting actions

Where governance inefficiencies are identified, the CISO shall initiate corrective actions and propose structural refinements.

### 7.10 Assumptions Applied

1. Nigerian executive leadership retains authority to approve and enforce governance decisions within scope.
2. Governance participants have sufficient competence and delegated authority to represent their functions.
3. Internal Audit remains independent from operational control ownership.
4. Governance tooling and reporting mechanisms are available to maintain auditable records.

## 7. Policy and Procedure (Communication Requirements)

### 7.1 Communication Principles

All ISMS communications shall be:

1. **Accurate:** Fact-based and verified before release.
2. **Timely:** Delivered within required governance, operational, and regulatory timelines.
3. **Relevant:** Tailored to recipient roles and need-to-know.
4. **Consistent:** Aligned with approved policies, procedures, and official positions.
5. **Protected:** Classified and transmitted through approved secure channels.
6. **Traceable:** Logged with evidence of approval, distribution, and acknowledgement where required.

### 7.2 Communication Objectives

The communication programme is designed to:

1. Promote awareness of security responsibilities and expected behaviors.
2. Support implementation and operation of ISMS controls.
3. Enable rapid escalation and coordinated response to incidents.
4. Demonstrate regulatory and audit readiness through documented communication records.
5. Ensure governance forums receive reliable decision-support information.
6. Maintain stakeholder trust through controlled and appropriate external communication.

### 7.5 Communication Channels and Security Controls

Approved communication channels include:

1. DTB corporate email (with security controls enforced)
2. Controlled intranet/document repository
3. Governance committee meetings (physical/secure virtual)
4. Incident management collaboration channels (restricted access)
5. Approved regulatory submission channels/portals
6. Contractual communications via procurement/legal channels

Mandatory controls for communications:

- Classification labeling on sensitive communications.
- Recipient validation prior to release.
- Encryption/protected channels for restricted data.
- Distribution lists maintained and reviewed.
- Prohibition of unauthorized personal messaging channels for official ISMS communications.

### 7.6 Approval and Authorization Rules

1. No external ISMS communication shall be released without designated approval authority.
2. Incident communications with potential legal, regulatory, or reputational impact require joint review by CISO, COMP, and DPO (where privacy is involved), with CEO approval where required.
3. Public-facing or media-facing communication is restricted to approved spokesperson channels.
4. Regulatory communications must be reviewed for factual accuracy and legal adequacy prior to submission.

### 7.7 Awareness and Acknowledgement Requirements

1. Mandatory security communications (policy updates, critical advisories, role obligations) shall require acknowledgement evidence where applicable.
2. Branch managers shall confirm cascade completion for branch-targeted directives.
3. HR and ISMS Programme Manager shall maintain communication completion and acknowledgement records.

### 7.8 Communication Record Retention

All ISMS communications requiring evidentiary traceability shall be retained in controlled repositories with metadata including:

- communication ID/type,
- sender/owner,
- approval evidence,
- recipients,
- date/time,
- classification,
- attachments/references,
- acknowledgement status (if required).

Retention shall align with DTB record control requirements and minimum periods defined in Section 8.

### 7.9 Escalation of Communication Failures

Where communication breakdown, delay, or inaccuracy could materially affect security outcomes:

1. Issue shall be logged by communication owner or recipient.
2. CISO shall assess impact and assign corrective action.
3. Regulatory-impacting communication failures shall be escalated immediately to COMP and CEO.
4. Recurrent failures shall be reviewed during management review and treated as potential nonconformities.

### 7.10 Assumptions Applied

1. DTB Nigeria has secure communication infrastructure available for internal and external ISMS communications.
2. Role owners have authority to issue communications within assigned mandates.
3. Supporting detailed procedures (Internal and External Communication Procedures) will be issued in Phase 4 and remain aligned with this plan.
