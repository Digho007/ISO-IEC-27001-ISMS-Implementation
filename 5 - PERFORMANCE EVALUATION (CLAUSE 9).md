## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to define the process for planning, conducting, reporting, and following up on internal audits of the Information Security Management System (ISMS) at DTB Nigeria. It ensures that internal audits provide objective information on whether the ISMS conforms to the organization's own requirements, the requirements of ISO/IEC 27001:2022, and whether it is effectively implemented and maintained.

This procedure ensures DTB Nigeria maintains reliable performance evaluation controls demonstrating ISMS conformity, operational effectiveness, and audit readiness in alignment with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.2.

## 3. Scope

This procedure applies to all internal audits conducted within the DTB Nigeria ISMS scope:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

It covers all processes, controls, departments, systems, and personnel included within the Statement of Applicability (SoA) and the formal ISMS scope.

Out-of-scope international entities (London, Frankfurt, Paris, New York) are excluded from this internal audit procedure, except where DTB Nigeria relies on services provided by these entities, which may be subject to review via service level agreements and supplier assurance evidence.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Audit Programme Planning

1. The Chief Internal Auditor, in consultation with the CISO and ISMS Programme Manager, shall establish an Annual Internal Audit Programme (DTB-NG-ISMS-AAP-057).
2. The audit programme shall be risk-based, taking into consideration the importance of the processes concerned, changes affecting the organization, and the results of previous audits.
3. Every process and control within the ISMS scope and SoA must be audited at least once over a three-year certification cycle, with high-risk areas audited annually.

### 7.2 Audit Preparation and Scope Definition

1. Prior to each specific audit, the Lead Auditor shall define the audit scope, criteria, and objectives.
2. The Lead Auditor shall select an audit team ensuring objectivity and the impartiality of the audit process. Auditors must not audit their own work.
3. An Audit Plan shall be drafted and communicated to the relevant process owners and auditees at least two (2) weeks prior to the audit execution, detailing the schedule, locations, and personnel required.

### 7.3 Conducting the Audit

1. **Opening Meeting:** The audit shall begin with an opening meeting to confirm the audit plan, scope, and communication channels.
2. **Evidence Gathering:** Auditors shall collect objective evidence through interviews, observation of activities, and review of documented information (e.g., logs, records, system configurations).
3. **Evaluation:** Evidence shall be evaluated against the audit criteria to determine conformity or nonconformity.
4. **Closing Meeting:** A closing meeting shall be held with the auditees and the CISO to present the preliminary findings, clarify any misunderstandings, and agree on the factual accuracy of the identified nonconformities.

### 7.4 Audit Reporting

1. The Lead Auditor shall prepare a formal Internal Audit Report detailing the audit scope, methodology, objective evidence reviewed, conformities, and any identified nonconformities or opportunities for improvement.
2. Nonconformities shall be clearly categorized (e.g., Major, Minor) and mapped to specific ISO 27001 clauses or internal DTB Nigeria ISMS policies.
3. The Internal Audit Report shall be distributed to the Chief Internal Auditor, the CISO, relevant Line Managers, and executive management.

### 7.5 Corrective Actions and Follow-up

1. Upon receipt of the Internal Audit Report, the CISO and relevant control owners shall initiate the Nonconformity Procedure (DTB-NG-ISMS-NCP-061) and Corrective Action Procedure (DTB-NG-ISMS-CAP-062).
2. Auditees must determine root causes, propose corrective actions, and define implementation timeframes.
3. Internal Audit shall conduct follow-up activities to verify the implementation and effectiveness of the corrective actions. Follow-up results shall be documented.
4. Unresolved or past-due corrective actions shall be escalated to the Information Security Management Committee (ISMC) and Managing Director/CEO.

### 7.6 Assumptions Applied

1. DTB Nigeria has a functionally independent Internal Audit department capable of objectively auditing the Information Security/IT functions.
2. Auditors possess the necessary competence and knowledge of the ISO/IEC 27001:2022 standard.
3. Executive management fully supports the internal audit mandate and enforces timely remediation of findings.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the schedule, frequency, methods, and responsibilities for conducting internal audits of the Information Security Management System (ISMS) at DTB Nigeria over a twelve-month period.

This programme ensures that internal audits are planned on a risk-based approach to objectively evaluate whether the ISMS conforms to organizational requirements and the ISO/IEC 27001:2022 standard, fulfilling the requirements of Clause 9.2.

## 3. Scope

This programme applies to all internal audit activities scheduled for the DTB Nigeria ISMS within the current calendar/financial year, covering:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

It encompasses all processes, departments, technologies, and physical locations included in the ISMS scope and the Statement of Applicability (SoA). Out-of-scope international entities are excluded unless they provide a direct, local service to DTB Nigeria that requires assurance.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Establishing the Audit Programme

1. The Chief Internal Auditor shall develop the Annual Internal Audit Programme during the fourth quarter (Q4) of the preceding year.
2. The programme formulation shall incorporate a risk-based approach, considering:
    - The current ISMS Risk Register (DTB-NG-ISMS-RRG-017) and highly rated risks.
    - The results and nonconformities from previous internal and external audits.
    - Significant changes to DTB Nigeria's IT infrastructure, business processes, or regulatory obligations (e.g., CBN directives).
    - The criticality of the processes and the Statement of Applicability (SoA).
3. The programme must ensure that every mandatory clause of ISO/IEC 27001:2022 (Clauses 4 through 10) and all applicable Annex A controls are audited at least once within a 3-year certification cycle.

### 7.2 Resource Allocation

1. The Chief Internal Auditor shall assign competent and impartial auditors to execute the programme.
2. Auditors must not audit their own work or processes they manage.
3. If internal resources lack specific technical expertise (e.g., advanced penetration testing, cloud architecture review), external subject matter experts or co-sourced auditors may be engaged to execute specific segments of the programme.

### 7.3 Programme Communication

1. Once approved, the Annual Internal Audit Programme shall be communicated to the ISMC, the CISO, and relevant Department Heads.
2. Specific audit dates within the programme are tentative and shall be confirmed by the Lead Auditor at least two weeks prior to the execution of each specific audit via a detailed Audit Plan.

### 7.4 Monitoring and Reviewing the Programme

1. The Chief Internal Auditor shall monitor the progress of the audit programme on a quarterly basis.
2. Adjustments to the programme (e.g., adding ad-hoc audits, changing schedules) may be made if significant security incidents occur, organizational structures change, or high-risk vulnerabilities are discovered.
3. The overall effectiveness of the audit programme shall be reported as an input to the ISMS Management Review.

### 7.5 Assumptions Applied

1. DTB Nigeria allocates an annual budget sufficient to resource the internal audit function or co-source external auditors where technical expertise is required.
2. Department heads are committed to supporting the audit schedule and providing required evidence in a timely manner.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this procedure is to define the process for executive management to review the Information Security Management System (ISMS) at planned intervals. This ensures its continuing suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness in managing information security risks and fulfilling the strategic objectives of DTB Nigeria.

This procedure ensures DTB Nigeria maintains reliable performance evaluation and governance controls demonstrating ISMS conformity, operational effectiveness, and alignment with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.3.

## 3. Scope

This procedure applies to the ISMS Management Review process conducted by the Information Security Management Committee (ISMC) and executive leadership within the DTB Nigeria ISMS scope:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

It covers the required inputs, expected outputs, scheduling, and record-keeping requirements for all formal Management Review meetings. Out-of-scope international entities are excluded from the local ISMC governance structure under this procedure.

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Frequency and Scheduling

1. Formal Management Review meetings shall be conducted at least **twice a year** (bi-annually).
2. Ad-hoc Management Reviews may be convened by the ISMC Chair or the CISO in the event of a critical security breach, significant organizational change, or a major shift in the regulatory landscape (e.g., new Central Bank of Nigeria mandates).
3. The ISMS Programme Manager shall schedule the meeting and distribute the agenda and input materials to all ISMC members at least one week prior to the meeting.

### 7.2 Management Review Inputs

The ISMS Programme Manager and CISO shall ensure that the following mandatory inputs are presented and discussed during the Management Review:

1. The status of actions from previous management reviews.
2. Changes in external and internal issues that are relevant to the ISMS.
3. Changes in needs and expectations of interested parties that are relevant to the ISMS.
4. Feedback on information security performance, including trends in:
    - Nonconformities and corrective actions.
    - Monitoring and measurement results.
    - Audit results (internal and external).
    - Fulfillment of information security objectives.
5. Feedback from interested parties.
6. Results of risk assessment and status of risk treatment plan.
7. Opportunities for continual improvement.

### 7.3 Management Review Outputs

1. Based on the evaluation of the inputs, the ISMC shall make decisions and authorize actions related to the ISMS.
2. The outputs of the management review shall include decisions related to:
    - Opportunities for continual improvement.
    - Any needs for changes to the Information Security Management System.
    - Resource needs (budget, personnel, technology).

### 7.4 Documenting the Review

1. The ISMS Programme Manager must record the proceedings, discussions, and formal decisions of the Management Review.
2. Minutes shall be documented using the Management Review Minutes Template (DTB-NG-ISMS-MRM-059).
3. The documented minutes must clearly capture the action items, responsible owners, and target completion dates.
4. The final minutes must be reviewed by the CISO and formally approved/signed by the Managing Director/CEO (ISMC Chair).

### 7.5 Assumptions Applied

1. The ISMC operates as the formal steering committee representing top management for all ISMS-related governance.
2. All required input data (metrics, audit reports, risk registers) is accurate, up-to-date, and consolidated by the CISO and ISMS Programme Manager prior to the meeting.
3. Resources approved during the management review will be subsequently provisioned through DTB Nigeria's standard procurement and HR processes.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the methodology and framework for monitoring, measuring, analyzing, and evaluating the information security performance and the effectiveness of the Information Security Management System (ISMS) at DTB Nigeria.

This framework ensures that DTB Nigeria determines what needs to be measured, the methods employed, the frequency of measurement, and who is responsible for analyzing the results, in alignment with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 9.1.

## 3. Scope

This framework applies to all objectives, processes, and controls within the DTB Nigeria ISMS scope:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

It encompasses both technical security metrics (e.g., system patching, incident response times) and management system metrics (e.g., audit completion rates, risk treatment progress). Out-of-scope international entities are excluded from this framework, except where their service delivery directly impacts DTB Nigeria's local KPIs.

## 5. Definitions

| **Term** | **Definition** |
| --- | --- |
| Key Performance Indicator (KPI) | A quantifiable measure used to evaluate the success of an organization, employee, or process in meeting objectives for performance. |
| Key Risk Indicator (KRI) | A metric used to provide an early signal of increasing risk exposures in various areas of the enterprise. |
| Metric | A standard of measurement that provides tracking data on a specific ISMS control or process. |
| Target | The specific value or threshold a KPI or Metric is expected to reach or maintain. |

## 6. Roles and Responsibilities

| **Role** | **Responsibilities** |
| --- | --- |
| CISO (Nigeria) | Approves the ISMS metrics, ensures they align with strategic business objectives, and presents the aggregated performance data to the ISMC. |
| ISMS Programme Manager | Defines the measurement methods, coordinates data collection from process owners, analyzes trends, and maintains the performance dashboard. |
| Control / Process Owners | Collect and provide accurate raw data and operational metrics for their respective domains (e.g., ITOPS for patch metrics, HR for training metrics). |
| Information Security Management Committee (ISMC) | Reviews the analyzed metrics during Management Reviews to make resource and strategic decisions. |

## 7. Procedure

### 7.1 Determining What Needs to be Measured

1. The CISO and ISMS Programme Manager shall establish metrics that directly tie to the formal Information Security Objectives (DTB-NG-ISMS-OBJ-009).
2. Metrics shall be defined to evaluate the effectiveness of critical security controls listed in the Statement of Applicability (SoA) and to track the operational performance of the ISMS processes (e.g., incident management, risk assessment, internal audit).
3. The selection of metrics must consider regulatory reporting requirements (e.g., Central Bank of Nigeria IT standards).

### 7.2 Defining Measurement Methods

1. For each identified metric, a specific measurement method must be documented to ensure data is collected consistently and produces comparable and reproducible results.
2. The method must define:
    - The data source (e.g., SIEM logs, HR training platform, vulnerability scanner).
    - The formula or calculation used (e.g., Number of patched servers / Total number of servers * 100).
    - The responsible data provider.

### 7.3 Frequency of Monitoring and Measurement

1. The frequency of data collection shall be appropriate to the nature of the control and the level of risk.
2. Measurement frequencies shall be categorized as:
    - **Continuous/Real-time:** (e.g., network availability, active threats blocked).
    - **Monthly:** (e.g., patch compliance, phishing click rates).
    - **Quarterly:** (e.g., access review completion, risk treatment progress).
    - **Annually:** (e.g., audit completion, BCP test success).

### 7.4 Analysis and Evaluation

1. The ISMS Programme Manager shall collate the measured data and conduct a formal analysis against the established Targets/Thresholds.
2. If a metric falls below the acceptable target or indicates a negative trend, the ISMS Programme Manager must initiate an investigation with the relevant Control Owner to determine the root cause.
3. Persistent failures to meet KPI targets shall trigger the Nonconformity Procedure (DTB-NG-ISMS-NCP-061) to implement formal corrective actions.

### 7.5 Reporting and Continuous Improvement

1. Analyzed metrics shall be compiled into an ISMS Performance Dashboard.
2. The dashboard shall be presented to the CISO monthly and to the ISMC during formal Management Review meetings.
3. The ISMC shall evaluate whether the current metrics remain valid or if new metrics need to be introduced based on changes in the organizational environment or threat landscape.

### 7.6 Assumptions Applied

1. DTB Nigeria utilizes automated tools (e.g., SIEM, GRC platforms, ITSM ticketing systems) to capture accurate raw data for technical and operational metrics.
2. Control owners provide truthful and verifiable data without manipulation.
3. Selected targets are realistic, achievable, and aligned with the bank's risk appetite.
