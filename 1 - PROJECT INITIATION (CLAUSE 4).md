## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this ISMS Project Charter is to formally authorize, govern, and resource the implementation of an Information Security Management System (ISMS) aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 for **Digho Trust Bank Nigeria operations only**.

This charter establishes project direction, governance, accountabilities, milestones, and deliverables required to achieve initial ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification readiness for the Nigerian scope.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Organizational Scope (In Scope)

This project applies to DTB Nigerian operations comprising:

- Head Office: Lagos
- Branches: Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan

### 3.2 Exclusions (Out of Scope)

The following DTB entities are explicitly excluded from this ISMS certification project:

- London Branch
- Frankfurt Branch
- Paris Representative Office
- New York Branch

### 3.3 ISMS Coverage

The project covers people, processes, information assets, and supporting technologies used to deliver banking services within Nigerian operations, including but not limited to:

- Retail Banking, Commercial Banking, Corporate & Investment Banking, Wealth Management, Digital Banking
- Active Directory, Microsoft Entra ID, Microsoft 365, Azure, Sentinel, Defender XDR, SentinelOne, Splunk
- Core Banking Platform, SWIFT connectivity, Corporate Banking Portal, DTB One Mobile Banking Platform, Internet Banking Platform
- On-premises and cloud infrastructure, endpoint estate, network services, and backup platforms

## 7. Project Charter Statement (Policy/Procedure Content)

### 7.1 Project Authorization

DTB formally authorizes the ISMS implementation project for Nigerian operations to establish, implement, maintain, and continually improve an ISO/IEC 27001:2022-compliant ISMS and obtain accredited third-party certification.

### 7.2 Business Justification

The ISMS project is initiated to:

1. Strengthen confidentiality, integrity, and availability of banking information assets.
2. Improve cyber resilience and operational trust in digital and branch banking channels.
3. Demonstrate regulatory and contractual assurance to CBN, NDPC, customers, and partners.
4. Standardize security governance across all in-scope Nigerian locations.
5. Reduce likelihood and impact of cyber incidents, fraud, and data compromise.
6. Provide external certification evidence to support market and stakeholder confidence.

### 7.3 Project Objectives

DTB shall deliver the following measurable objectives:

1. Define and approve ISMS context, scope, and governance for Nigeria by project Month 2.
2. Complete risk assessment, risk treatment plan, and SoA aligned to Annex A:2022 by Month 5.
3. Implement and/or formalize required policies, procedures, and operational controls by Month 8.
4. Complete ISMS awareness and role-based training for all in-scope staff by Month 9.
5. Conduct at least one full internal ISMS audit cycle and management review before certification audit by Month 10.
6. Achieve Stage 1 and Stage 2 audit readiness with objective evidence by Month 12.

### 7.4 Assumptions

The project is based on the following assumptions:

1. Nigerian executive leadership will provide sustained sponsorship and funding.
2. Process owners and control owners will allocate qualified personnel and timely evidence.
3. Existing security technologies (e.g., Sentinel, Splunk, Defender XDR, SentinelOne) remain operational and available for control monitoring evidence.
4. Overseas branches will cooperate on interface-level information exchange requirements where needed, but remain out of scope for certification evidence.
5. Certification body selection and audit windows will be finalized no later than Month 8.

### 7.5 Constraints

1. Certification scope is restricted to Nigeria and must remain clearly segregated from non-Nigerian operations.
2. Project must not disrupt critical banking operations, SWIFT services, or customer-facing digital channels.
3. Regulatory obligations under Nigerian law and CBN directives take precedence in control interpretation for this scope.
4. Documentation and implementation quality must be sufficient for external audit scrutiny by accredited certification bodies.

### 7.6 High-Level Deliverables

The project shall produce, at minimum:

- ISO/IEC 27001:2022-mandated ISMS documentation set (as per implementation roadmap)
- Risk assessment methodology and risk register
- Risk treatment plan and Statement of Applicability
- Implemented operational controls with evidence repositories
- Internal audit records, nonconformity/corrective action records
- Management review outputs
- Certification readiness dossier

### 7.7 Governance and Reporting

1. A monthly ISMS Steering Committee meeting shall review status, risks, dependencies, and decisions.
2. A bi-weekly project working group shall track execution progress and blockers.
3. A formal monthly dashboard shall report:
    - Milestone status (RAG)
    - Key risks/issues and mitigation actions
    - Training completion metrics
    - Control implementation progress
    - Audit readiness index

### 7.9 Success Criteria

Project success is achieved when all conditions are met:

1. ISMS documentation is approved, controlled, and implemented across all in-scope Nigerian sites.
2. Identified risks are assessed, treated, and tracked according to approved criteria.
3. Mandatory ISO clauses and applicable Annex A controls are demonstrably operating.
4. Internal audit and management review are completed with corrective actions progressed.
5. DTB Nigeria is recommended for ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification by the selected accredited certification body (subject to closure of any minor findings).

### 7.10 Risk and Issue Management (Project-Level)

- A centralized RAID log (Risks, Assumptions, Issues, Dependencies) shall be maintained by the ISMS Programme Manager.
- High and critical risks shall be escalated within 2 business days to the CISO and Steering Committee Chair.
- Regulatory-impacting issues shall trigger Compliance and Legal review within 24 hours.

### 7.11 Change Control

Any material change to project scope, objectives, timelines, or certification boundaries shall require:

1. Impact assessment (security, regulatory, operational, budgetary)
2. Steering Committee review
3. Formal approval by CISO and Executive Sponsor
4. Revision to this charter and communication to all affected stakeholders

## 7. Context of the Organization

### 7.1 Organization Profile and Strategic Direction

Digho Trust Bank (DTB) is an international financial institution with headquarters in Lagos, Nigeria. For this certification initiative, DTB has elected to implement an independent ISMS for Nigerian operations only.

DTB Nigeria’s strategic direction includes:

1. Expansion of secure digital banking channels (mobile and internet banking).
2. Protection of customer trust through resilient financial operations.
3. Regulatory compliance with CBN and Nigerian statutory obligations.
4. Risk-informed growth across retail, commercial, corporate, and wealth segments.
5. Strengthened cyber resilience for critical banking and payment services.

The ISMS shall support these strategic priorities by embedding information security governance, risk management, and control assurance into business and technology operations.

### 7.2 Clause 4.1 — Internal Issues

The following internal issues are relevant to DTB Nigeria’s purpose and strategic direction and affect the ISMS outcome:

| **Internal Issue Category** | **DTB Nigeria Context** | **ISMS Relevance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Governance Structure | Nigerian operations are managed through local executive leadership, risk committees, and centralized control functions | Enables formal accountability for information security decisions and approvals |
| Organization Size & Distribution | Approx. 580 global employees; Nigerian scope includes multiple branches and head office | Requires consistent control implementation and evidence collection across locations |
| Service Complexity | Multi-line banking services (retail, commercial, CIB, wealth, digital) | Increases asset criticality, process interdependencies, and attack surface |
| Technology Landscape | Hybrid enterprise architecture across on-prem and cloud platforms (AD, Entra ID, M365, Azure, SIEM, EDR, core banking) | Necessitates layered controls for identity, monitoring, segmentation, and recovery |
| Critical Systems Dependence | Reliance on core banking platform, SWIFT connectivity, digital channels | Demands high availability, secure transaction processing, and incident response readiness |
| Security Culture Maturity | Mixed maturity across technical teams, operations, and branch personnel | Requires structured competence, awareness, and role-based control ownership |
| Third-Party Reliance | Use of suppliers for software, infrastructure, telecoms, and managed services | Requires robust supplier risk management and contractual security controls |
| Business Continuity Requirements | Banking operations require minimal disruption tolerance | Drives strong backup, resilience, DR, and crisis escalation controls |
| Documentation & Process Standardization | Need to harmonize branch and head-office procedures under one Nigeria ISMS | Necessitates controlled documentation, versioning, and audit evidence discipline |

### 7.5 Clause 4.3 — Determining the Scope of the ISMS (Context Basis)

In determining ISMS scope, DTB Nigeria considered:

1. Internal and external issues identified in sections 7.2 and 7.3.
2. Requirements of interested parties identified in section 7.4.
3. Interfaces and dependencies between Nigerian operations and out-of-scope international entities.
4. Regulatory obligations applicable to Nigerian financial operations.

**Scope determination outcome:**

The ISMS includes all Nigerian offices listed in Section 3 and excludes overseas offices listed in Section 3, while governing security controls for information exchanges between in-scope and out-of-scope entities where applicable.

### 7.6 Clause 4.4 — ISMS Establishment, Implementation, Maintenance, Continual Improvement

Based on established context, DTB Nigeria shall:

1. Establish and maintain a risk-based ISMS aligned to ISO/IEC 27001:2022.
2. Define risk criteria and treatment approaches proportionate to banking risk exposure.
3. Implement Annex A and additional controls as required by risk and regulation.
4. Monitor, measure, audit, and review ISMS effectiveness.
5. Continually improve ISMS suitability, adequacy, and effectiveness through corrective actions and management review.

### 7.7 Context Monitoring and Review Method

DTB Nigeria shall review context at least annually and upon significant change triggers, including:

- New or revised CBN/NDPC requirements
- Major cyber incidents (internal or sector-wide)
- Significant technology transformation (e.g., platform migration)
- Mergers, branch restructuring, or operating model changes
- Material supplier or outsourcing changes
- Changes to cross-border data exchange interfaces

The ISMS Programme Manager shall maintain a **Context Review Log**, with outputs fed into risk assessment updates, policy updates, and management review.

### 7.8 Assumptions and Boundaries Applied

The following assumptions were used in this context analysis:

1. Nigerian operations maintain operational control over in-scope systems and processes.
2. Out-of-scope branches are governed independently and are not subject to this ISMS certification evidence set.
3. Shared group services used by Nigeria are subject to local control validation before reliance in ISMS evidence.
4. Technology stack listed in project documentation reflects current Nigeria operating baseline as of 01 July 2026.

- Can influence certification outcomes or assurance decisions.

### 7.4 Determination of Mandatory vs Voluntary Requirements

DTB Nigeria classifies requirements as:

1. **Mandatory Requirements:** Legal, regulatory, and binding contractual obligations.Non-fulfillment may result in sanctions, legal exposure, financial penalties, or certification impact.
2. **Committed Requirements:** Governance commitments, board directives, customer assurance commitments, and internal standards adopted by DTB.

Both categories are included in ISMS planning, risk treatment, and performance monitoring.

### 7.5 Integration into ISMS Processes

Interested party requirements are integrated into the ISMS through:

- ISMS scope and context definitions
- Risk assessment criteria and risk treatment decisions
- Control selection and SoA justifications
- Policy and procedure framework
- Supplier due diligence and contracting controls
- Incident response and regulatory reporting workflows
- Internal audit and management review inputs

### 7.6 Escalation and Change Management for Requirements

Where a new or changed interested party requirement is identified:

1. Requirement is logged by Compliance/Legal or relevant owner.
2. Applicability and impact assessment is performed within 10 business days.
3. Required updates are assigned to policy/control owners.
4. Risk register and treatment plan are updated where relevant.
5. Changes are reported at ISMS Steering Committee and recorded in management review inputs.

Urgent regulatory directives (e.g., CBN/NDPC) are escalated immediately and treated under expedited compliance action.

### 7.7 Assumptions Applied

1. All in-scope DTB Nigeria business units cooperate in providing evidence against relevant requirements.
2. Out-of-scope international entities are treated as external interface stakeholders for this ISMS.
3. Regulatory obligations referenced remain current as of 01 July 2026 and are subject to continuous monitoring.

## 2. Purpose

The purpose of this document is to define the formal scope boundaries of the Information Security Management System (ISMS) for Digho Trust Bank (DTB) Nigeria in accordance with ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Clause 4.3.

This scope statement establishes organizational, physical, technological, process, and information boundaries for certification and ensures clarity for internal stakeholders, auditors, regulators, customers, and relevant third parties.

## 3. Scope

### 3.1 Official ISMS Scope Statement

The ISMS of Digho Trust Bank (DTB) applies to the governance, processes, people, information, applications, and supporting infrastructure used to deliver banking and financial services within DTB Nigeria operations, including Head Office (Lagos) and branches in Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, and Ibadan.

The scope includes retail banking, commercial banking, corporate and investment banking, wealth management, and digital banking services, supported by on-premises and cloud-based technology platforms, including identity and access management, endpoint security, network security, monitoring and logging, incident response, backup and recovery, supplier security management, and secure information exchange interfaces.

The scope excludes DTB operations in London, Frankfurt, Paris, and New York, which are independently governed and outside this certification boundary. Where cross-border information exchange occurs with these out-of-scope entities, interface controls, contractual obligations, and boundary protections are managed within the DTB Nigeria ISMS.

### 3.2 Organizational Boundary (In Scope)

The following DTB Nigeria organizational units and functions are in scope:

- Executive Management (Nigeria)
- Information Security (GRC and SOC functions in Nigeria)
- IT Infrastructure and Operations (Nigeria)
- Digital Banking Technology and Operations (Nigeria)
- Core Banking Application Support (Nigeria)
- Branch Operations (Lagos, Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan)
- Compliance and Legal (Nigeria)
- Data Protection Office (Nigeria)
- Human Resources (Nigeria) for security competence and awareness controls
- Procurement/Vendor Management (Nigeria) for supplier security controls
- Internal Audit (Nigeria) for ISMS internal audit activities

### 3.3 Physical Boundary (In Scope)

1. Head Office – Lagos
2. Branch – Abuja
3. Branch – Port Harcourt
4. Branch – Enugu
5. Branch – Calabar
6. Branch – Ibadan

Physical areas covered include office floors, branch operational areas, controlled server/network rooms (where present), secure document storage, and access-controlled technology facilities used by Nigerian operations.

### 3.4 Technological Boundary (In Scope)

The ISMS scope includes security governance and operation for the following technology components used by Nigerian operations:

- Active Directory
- Microsoft Entra ID
- Microsoft 365 services in use by Nigeria
- Microsoft Azure resources used by Nigeria
- Microsoft Sentinel
- Microsoft Defender XDR
- SentinelOne EDR
- Splunk (where deployed for log analytics/security use cases)
- Core Banking Platform (Nigeria operational instance/use)
- SWIFT connectivity components managed/operated for Nigeria
- Corporate Banking Portal
- DTB One Mobile Banking Platform
- Internet Banking Platform
- Windows servers
- Linux servers
- SQL databases
- Endpoint encryption services
- Secure backup and restore infrastructure
- Corporate VPN services
- Firewalls and Cisco network infrastructure supporting Nigeria operations

### 3.5 Information Assets (In Scope)

Information categories covered by this ISMS include, but are not limited to:

- Customer personally identifiable and financial account information
- Payment and transaction data
- Credit and lending information
- Treasury and corporate transaction records
- Employee and contractor information relevant to HR/operations
- Security logs, monitoring records, and incident records
- Regulatory and compliance records
- Supplier and contractual security records
- Business continuity and disaster recovery records
- Internal policies, procedures, and audit evidence

### 3.6 Process Boundary (In Scope)

Core ISMS-relevant processes in scope include:

1. Information security governance and risk management
2. Asset management and information classification
3. Identity and access management (including privileged access governance)
4. Cryptographic and data protection controls
5. Endpoint, server, and network security operations
6. Logging, monitoring, and threat detection
7. Vulnerability and patch management
8. Incident management and response
9. Backup, restoration, business continuity, and disaster recovery
10. Secure supplier lifecycle management
11. Secure change management
12. Security awareness, competence, and communications
13. Internal audit, management review, corrective action, and continual improvement

### 3.7 Products and Services Covered

The scope covers information security controls supporting delivery of:

- Retail Banking services
- Commercial Banking services
- Corporate & Investment Banking services
- Wealth Management services
- Digital Banking services (mobile and internet channels)
- Related customer support and operational processing services within Nigeria scope

### 3.8 Interfaces and Dependencies

DTB Nigeria’s ISMS recognizes and controls critical interfaces/dependencies with:

- Out-of-scope DTB international entities (London/Frankfurt/Paris/New York) for approved business information exchange
- Correspondent banks and financial counterparties
- SWIFT ecosystem participants and service dependencies
- Payment processors/switches/clearing networks (as applicable)
- Cloud, telecom, security, and managed service suppliers
- Regulatory and legal reporting channels

For each dependency, DTB Nigeria applies proportionate controls such as contractual security obligations, access restrictions, encryption, logging, incident notification requirements, and periodic assurance reviews.

### 3.9 Out-of-Scope Statement

The following are explicitly out of scope for this ISO/IEC 27001:2022 certification boundary:

- London Branch operations, assets, systems, personnel, and internal processes
- Frankfurt Branch operations, assets, systems, personnel, and internal processes
- Paris Representative Office operations, assets, systems, personnel, and internal processes
- New York Branch operations, assets, systems, personnel, and internal processes

These entities maintain independent governance structures and will pursue separate certification initiatives. Nonconformities in those entities shall not be treated as direct certification nonconformities for DTB Nigeria, except where a Nigeria-controlled interface is inadequately managed.

### 3.10 Justification for Scope Boundaries and Exclusions

DTB has defined a Nigeria-only ISMS certification boundary to:

1. Align certification governance with local executive control and local regulatory obligations.
2. Ensure accountability and auditability of controls under Nigerian management authority.
3. Prevent cross-regional certification dependency risk.
4. Enable phased certification by regional operating entity.
5. Improve practicality of evidence collection, internal audit execution, and corrective action closure within a controlled boundary.

Exclusions are not intended to omit required controls; rather, they reflect legal entity and governance boundaries. Relevant external interface risks are included in the Nigeria ISMS risk treatment process.

### 3.11 Applicability to ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Requirements

All ISO/IEC 27001:2022 clauses are applicable to this ISMS scope.

Control applicability is defined in the Statement of Applicability (SoA), including controls selected from Annex A and additional controls identified through risk treatment.

1. Nigerian operations retain management authority over in-scope processes and assets.

3. Cross-border interfaces are formally governed through approved processes and contractual controls.
4. Technology baseline listed in this document is accurate as of 01 July 2026 and subject to controlled updates.

### 7.4 End-to-End Interaction Flow (Narrative)

1. **Context, scope, and interested party needs** define security obligations and priorities.
2. These obligations feed **risk assessment**, producing a risk register.
3. Risks are translated into **risk treatment actions and SoA decisions**.
4. Approved controls are operationalized through:
    - asset and classification governance,
    - IAM/PAM,
    - secure configuration,
    - vulnerability/patching,
    - logging/monitoring,
    - supplier controls.
5. Operational outputs trigger **incident response and resilience processes** (backup/DR/BCM).
6. Process outputs are measured through **KPI monitoring and internal audits**.
7. Findings, trends, and changes are evaluated in **management review**.
8. Resulting decisions trigger **corrective actions and continual improvement**, feeding back into context/risk/planning.

### 7.6 Process Performance and Interaction KPIs

To ensure interaction effectiveness, DTB Nigeria tracks cross-process KPIs including:

1. Percentage of high-risk findings with approved treatment plans within SLA.
2. Percentage of critical assets with assigned owners and classification labels.
3. User access recertification completion rate by due date.
4. Mean time to detect (MTTD) and mean time to respond (MTTR) for security incidents.
5. Vulnerability remediation SLA adherence (critical/high severity).
6. Backup success and restoration test success rates.
7. Internal audit finding closure rate within agreed timelines.
8. Corrective action effectiveness pass rate at verification review.
9. Percentage of management review actions completed by due date.

### 7.7 Control of Process Interfaces

To prevent control breakdown at handoffs, DTB Nigeria mandates:

1. Defined process inputs/outputs and ownership for each ISMS process.
2. Formal SLAs or internal OLAs for time-sensitive interactions.
3. Escalation path for blocked dependencies (technical, business, compliance).
4. Evidence retention for each process handoff.
5. Periodic interface effectiveness review during ISMS Steering Committee sessions.

### 7.8 Process Interaction Review and Change Control

Process interactions shall be reviewed:

- Quarterly by ISMS Programme Manager and process owners.
- During management review for suitability and adequacy.
- Immediately after major incidents, significant audit findings, or major technology/organizational changes.

Any material interaction change requires impact assessment and documented approval by CISO.

### 7.9 Assumptions Applied

1. All Nigerian process owners maintain required documented procedures and records.
2. Shared technology platforms used by Nigeria can provide adequate evidence for in-scope controls.
3. Interactions with out-of-scope branches are limited to controlled interface points governed by Nigeria process owners.

## 7. Organizational Structure (Policy/Procedure Content)

### 7.1 Organizational Design Principles for ISMS

DTB Nigeria’s ISMS organizational structure is based on the following principles:

1. **Clear Accountability:** Each ISMS process and control has a designated accountable role.
2. **Segregation of Duties:** Governance, operation, and assurance roles are separated to reduce conflict of interest.
3. **Risk-Based Decisioning:** Authority levels correspond to risk criticality and regulatory implications.
4. **Three Lines Model Alignment:** Business/operations, oversight functions, and independent assurance are structurally distinct.
5. **Branch Inclusion:** Branch operations are embedded in one Nigeria-wide ISMS, with local accountability and central governance.
6. **Auditability:** Reporting lines and decision authorities are documented and evidenced.

### 7.2 High-Level Governance Hierarchy

DTB Nigeria’s ISMS governance hierarchy is defined as follows:

1. Board Risk & Compliance Committee (Nigeria Oversight)
2. Managing Director/CEO (Nigeria)
3. Chief Information Security Officer (CISO), Nigeria
4. ISMS Steering Committee (Cross-functional management forum)
5. Functional Security and Control Owners (IT, SOC, Compliance, DPO, HR, Procurement, Branch Operations)
6. Operational Teams and Branch Control Executors
7. Independent Internal Audit (separate assurance function)

4. **Unresolved systemic weaknesses** escalate to Board Risk & Compliance Committee through executive reporting.

### 7.6 Branch-Specific Responsibility Clarification

To ensure consistent control operation across all in-scope locations (Abuja, Port Harcourt, Enugu, Calabar, Ibadan):

1. Branch Operations Managers are **Responsible (R)** for local execution of approved controls (physical security enforcement, local access practices, awareness completion, incident reporting, evidence submission).
2. Control accountability remains centralized with designated Nigeria-wide accountable roles (e.g., CISO, ITOPS, SOC, COMP), ensuring one-point accountability for certification.
3. Branch nonconformities are escalated through CISO governance channels and tracked in corrective action registers.

### 7.7 RACI Governance and Maintenance Rules

1. This RACI matrix is a controlled document and audit evidence artifact.
2. Any role or process change affecting assignments requires:
    - documented impact review,
    - CISO approval,
    - communication to affected stakeholders,
    - update of related procedures.
3. RACI conflicts or ambiguity shall be resolved by CISO and escalated to CEO where unresolved.
4. RACI effectiveness shall be reviewed during management review and internal audits.

### 7.8 Assumptions Applied

1. Role titles represent functional accountability even where named incumbents may change.
2. Delegation of tasks is permitted, but delegation of accountability (A) is not permitted unless formally approved and documented.
3. Matrix reflects Nigeria ISMS structure as of 01 July 2026 and excludes internal governance of out-of-scope international branches.
